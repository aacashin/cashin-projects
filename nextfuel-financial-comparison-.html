Strengths

What each agency brings

Three Sixty

Sofitel Riyadh · Al Ameerah Nouf Hall
  • Broader operational cover: event insurance, permits, full electrical support and crowd management — items that fall on us if the agency doesn't carry them.
  • Four full days in the hall: two for build and rehearsal, one for the event, one for dismantle — real room for a full run-through.
  • Capacity up to 250 guests with no re-pricing needed.
  • Programme and reporting priced sensibly at SAR 50,000 against SAR 143,000 from HROOF.
  • Larger physical production: 17m × 5m LED screen and a 15m × 5m main stage.
  • Comfortable payment terms: 50% on approval, 50% two weeks after the event date.
  • Live instrumental music included in the scope.

HROOF

Hilton Riyadh · Al Ula Hall
  • Less than half the cost, and the price is fixed for 120 days rather than estimated.
  • An agency we already work with that knows the CASHIN identity — no learning curve.
  • Media production included: two videographers, two photographers and a promo film — a marketing asset that outlives the event.
  • Clear breakdown by quantity and unit, which makes line-by-line negotiation straightforward.
  • Materially better hospitality: 8 kg stuffed dates, 8 kg premium chocolate, and 11 coffee service staff.
  • Custom décor in CASHIN identity, including table numbering and a 244 × 244 photo wall.
  • Hilton Riyadh is central and familiar to the B2B audience we're inviting.
Points requiring attention

Issues to raise with each agency

These are the questions to put to both parties before any approval, and they form the core of our negotiating position.

On the Three Sixty bid

  1. The venue alone is 52% of the total. SAR 195,000 × 4 days means SAR 585,000 is paid for three days with no event on them. Hotels normally price build days at a fraction of the event-day rate — compare the Hilton extra setup day at SAR 40,250.
  2. Venue and catering cost per guest is roughly SAR 3,467 against SAR 690 at the Hilton — a fivefold difference.
  3. VIP seating at SAR 450 per chair × 200 = SAR 90,000. Clearly overpriced.
  4. No media production at all — no photography, video or promo film, which is a core requirement for us.
  5. The notes state twice that prices are estimated and may be affected until final approval — an open-ended risk of cost escalation.
  6. Date conflict: the event date field reads October 2026 while the notes reference 19–22 September.
  7. The agency is Jeddah-based delivering in Riyadh, adding travel, logistics and execution risk.

On the HROOF bid

  1. Missing: event insurance, permits and electrical distribution. These need to be requested in writing with responsibility clearly assigned.
  2. Capacity is 150 only — if the target is 200+, the bid needs re-pricing.
  3. Programme design at SAR 110,499.60 plus reporting at SAR 32,499.60 comes to SAR 143,000, or 20% of the bid, against SAR 50,000 from Three Sixty. This is our single biggest negotiation lever.
  4. Most line items are exactly 1.15× a round base figure (600 → 690 · 720 → 828 · 84,000 → 96,600 · 16,200 → 18,630), which exposes a 15% agency margin applied before VAT. Ask for the margin to be disclosed or discounted.
  5. Line 9 — audio, lighting and visual systems at SAR 96,600 — carries no specification. Equipment list and screen dimensions must be fixed in writing.

What to request from HROOF

SAR 143,000 currently sits behind two undescribed lines
  • Break "programme design" into named deliverables with a price against each.
  • A draft minute-by-minute run of show as a sample, before signature.
  • Number of speakers covered, and whether their fees are inside the price.
  • Master of ceremonies — included, or quoted separately and at what rate.
  • LED content: how many minutes of motion graphics and how many versions.
  • Number of revision rounds included at no extra cost.
  • Ownership of open source files after the event.

What to request from Three Sixty

SAR 25,000 looks low enough to question
  • The same deliverable-by-deliverable breakdown, with prices.
  • Clarification of whether the described scope is a contractual commitment or marketing language.
  • Confirmation that media production can be added, and at what cost.
  • A corrected quote resolving the September / October date conflict.
  • Written confirmation of which prices are fixed and which remain estimates.
Recommendation

Award to HROOF — subject to conditions

The SAR 835,000 gap on equalised capacity is not justified by any difference in value. 80% of it traces to a single line: the cost of the room at the Sofitel — not the standard of delivery. On top of that, the Three Sixty bid omits media production entirely and its pricing is explicitly unfixed and free to rise up to the moment of approval.

The HROOF bid is fixed for 120 days, includes media production, and would be delivered by a team already fluent in the CASHIN identity. Its gaps — insurance, permits and electrical — can be added at a known cost, whereas the venue premium in the Three Sixty bid is not meaningfully negotiable. Neither bid contains an actual programme, so the run of show, MC and screen content must be pinned down in writing before either is signed. We use the Three Sixty bid as leverage: its SAR 50,000 programme and reporting line becomes our market reference against HROOF.
